# Abstract (draft, IEEE QCE 2026)

**QFA-LTL: Noise-Adaptive Statistical Test Oracles for Temporal Verification of NISQ Circuit Executions**

Verifying the behavior of quantum circuits on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices is complicated by stochastic device noise: a correct circuit can fail a static test threshold on a bad calibration day, while a faulty circuit can pass one on a good day. We present QFA-LTL, a framework that synthesizes *noise-adaptive statistical test oracles* for quantum circuit executions. Before each run, a zero-knowledge-style calibration probe (a Bell-state fidelity measurement on the physical qubits the circuit maps to) estimates the current device noise ε; a volume-based anchor then derives a circuit-specific pass/fail threshold from ε and the circuit's two-qubit-gate volume. Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) specifications — including `F`, `G`, bounded variants, and multi-basis probability predicates — define what "acceptable measured behavior" means, and are evaluated over measurement windows as windowed Bernoulli estimates, making temporal properties well-defined on shot histograms.

We evaluate the framework on a 24-circuit benchmark suite (six quantum algorithms, each with three injected fault classes) executed under IBM Brisbane calibration noise models via Aer. Results: a 0.0% false-failure rate across all correct circuits (the failure mode the framework targets), 100% alarm precision, and 77.8% bug-detection recall. We show that the 22% of undetected faults are *measurement-invisible by construction* — they preserve the target-basis probability distribution (phase errors, distribution-preserving entanglement mutations) — and characterize this detectability frontier. The circuit-to-automaton compiler is validated against exact statevector simulation to machine precision (max |Δp| ≈ 9e-16), with a tracked truncation-error bound for state-space control.

Our contribution is a principled, reproducible oracle-synthesis pipeline for the question classical equivalence checking does not answer: *is the noisy measured behavior of this circuit acceptable against its temporal specification?*
